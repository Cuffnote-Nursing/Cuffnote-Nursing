# Cuffnote Study Disclaimer

*Effective October 3, 2026 · version 2026-10-03*

## Study aid only

Cuffnote is an educational tool that helps nursing students study. It is **not** medical advice, **not** a clinical decision-support tool, and it must **never** be used to diagnose, treat or make decisions about a real patient.

## Always verify

Everything in Cuffnote (study guides, flashcards, drug sheets, lab values, ECG lessons, practice questions, cartoon visuals, calculators and AI answers) may contain mistakes, may be simplified, and may be out of date. Always check it against credible, current sources before you rely on it, such as:

- your instructors and course materials, and your nursing textbooks;
- your facility's policies and procedures, and the orders of the prescriber;
- current evidence-based clinical guidelines and professional organisations;
- official drug labels, a current drug reference, or a pharmacist.

If anything in Cuffnote disagrees with your instructor, your facility's policy or an official source, **follow them, not the app**.

## Medications, calculators and ECGs

- Doses, ranges, normal values and calculators are teaching aids. Verify every dose and calculation independently and follow your facility's double-check rules.
- ECG strips in Cuffnote are computer-generated teaching examples. They are not recordings of real patients and are not a substitute for formal ECG training.
- Cartoons and mnemonics simplify ideas on purpose. They leave out detail.

## Practice questions and exams

The practice questions are original and written for studying. They are not real NCLEX questions, and Cuffnote is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN) or any school or testing organisation. NCLEX is a registered trademark of NCSBN. Using Cuffnote does not guarantee that you will pass any course or exam.

## AI tutor

AI answers are generated by third-party models and can be wrong, incomplete or invented, even when they sound sure. Treat them as a starting point and verify them. **Never type patient information** (names, dates of birth, record numbers, photos or anything that could identify a person) into Cuffnote or into the AI.
